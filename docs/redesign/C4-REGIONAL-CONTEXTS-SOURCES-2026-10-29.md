## Đông Sơn: different contexts for drums

The renewed [Met miniature record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39209), `met-39209`, identifies **2000.284.57**, bronze, Vietnam attribution, approximately 500 BCE–300 CE and **10.2 cm** high. Consultation covered title, Description paragraphs 1–2, Artwork Details and Public Domain statement. The provenance tab was not independently opened. The visible record supplies no excavated findspot or secure burial context for this miniature. Broader drums’ proposed functions do not establish its use. Its existing **1600×1204** photograph was actually inspected; all four frog forms, decorated surfaces and flared base can be observed without supplying a sound, owner or original ritual. The Met Open Access policy was separately checked for CC0; no new remote-original byte comparison or photography date is asserted.

Nguyen Van Doan’s [2014 museum account](https://baotanglichsuquocgia.vn/en/Articles/3188/16665/dong-son-culture-in-vietnam-90-years-of-discovery-and-study-part-1.html), translated by Tran Trang and published **28 July 2014**, is `dongson-vnmh-2014`. Selected opening chronology, sections 1 and 2, and byline were read. It distinguishes Red, Mã and Cả river settings and an archaeological-culture sequence from the seventh century BCE through the first/second centuries CE. National continuity, progressive rankings and state-origin claims are not adopted as recovered political facts. No distribution image was reuse-cleared.

Two official Ngọc Lũ accounts conflict. Dương Hà’s [27 February 2013 record](https://vnmh.com.vn/en/Articles/1004/13749/the-ngoc-lu-drum-lsb-5722.html), `dongson-ngoc-lu-2013`, gives a third–second-century BCE convention and places discovery at Ngọc Lũ in 1893–1894, followed by use at Như Trác. The museum’s [3 January 2023 record](https://vnmh.com.vn/en/Articles/1004/73640/national-treasures-the-ngoc-lu-drum.html), `dongson-ngoc-lu-2023`, instead places embankment-work discovery at Như Trác in 1893 and later communal-house use at Ngọc Lũ. Both refer to **LSb.5722**. The malformed English date in the 2023 page is unusable and is not silently translated into a corrected calendar interval. Neither account authorizes an exact find pit, a connecting route or one universal ceremonial use. Museum photographs are not reused.

The [Cổ Loa record](https://thanhcoloa.vn/en/co-loa-bronze-drum), `dongson-co-loa-2021`, was published **23 December 2021**. Selected opening find/deposition text records a 1982 Mả Tre group, with the drum described as buried upside down and containing tools, weapons and other objects. Its broad “over 2,000 BP” convention does not become an exact ancient event. No owner, army, recycling purpose or ritual is assigned from that group.

### Phú Chánh and later production

Bui Chi Hoang’s [Phú Chánh report](https://journals.lib.washington.edu/index.php/BIPPA/article/view/12017), `dongson-phu-chanh-2008`, is _Bulletin of the Indo-Pacific Prehistory Association_ **28 (2008), 67–72**, DOI **10.7152/bippa.v28i0.12017**, published **19 June 2008**. The six-page institutional PDF was obtained. Selected text and actual page pixels were inspected at **printed pp.67, 69, 70 and 72 / PDF pp.1, 3, 4 and 6**; the layouts of PDF pp.2 and 5 were not inspected. The title in the PDF includes “later,” which the landing-page title omits.

The report distinguishes farmers’ discoveries, excavation in 2000 and survey in 2001. Pit 6 describes a drum above a wooden jar. Other evidence includes wooden tools reinterpreted as weaving equipment. The pits are **presumed graves**: no bones survived in the acidic conditions. Neither the tool forms nor the burial interpretation identifies an individual’s gender or occupation. Four wood results—**2100±150, 1850±55, 1910±60 and 1750±70 BP**—are reported without a supplied calibrated calendar convention. They do not establish one deposition year for all pits. Summary categories and individual pit accounts differ on wooden jars; the specific Pit 6 account takes precedence over an inferred inventory. The article’s movement/trade interpretation supplies no demonstrated route or political control. No explicit reusable figure licence was found; inspected figures are research evidence, not copied or traced media.

The Department of Cultural Heritage’s undated [Phú Chánh treasure record](https://dsvh.gov.vn/mo-chum-go-nap-trong-dong-phu-chanh-3088), `dongson-phu-chanh-treasure`, was read for its material/dimensions/date block and selected description. It gives a second–first-century BCE drum convention and a **2100±40-year** wood result. That result differs from the 2008 report and is not substituted for it. Its drum-face measurement is **47.5 cm**, versus **47.2 cm** in the excavation report; the distinction is retained. Rank/wealth and first-in-world claims are not adopted. No image-reuse licence was established.

The museum’s [17 January 2016 Luy Lâu account](https://baotanglichsuquocgia.vn/vi/Articles/3090/18909/khai-quat-di-tich-thanh-co-luy-lau-nam-2015-nhung-ket-qua-quan-trong.html), `dongson-luy-lau-2016`, was read for mould/casting-equipment paragraphs, chronological assessments and byline. Photographs credited Chu Mạnh Quyền have no verified reusable licence.

Lê Cảnh Lam and Trương Đắc Chiến’s [9 July 2026 analytical account](https://vass.gov.vn/nghien-cuu-khoa-hoc/ung-dung-cac-phuong-phap-khoa-hoc-tu-nhien-nghien-cuu-khuon-duc-trong-dong-luy-lau-bac-ninh-577452), `dongson-luy-lau-material-analysis-2026`, is a selected research lead, not a universal casting recipe. Technical tables were visible but not independently reanalysed; temperature/chemical causal claims are unnecessary to the bounded account. No figure copying is cleared.

The [2019 Timor-Leste paper](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2018.177), `dongson-timor-2019-abstract`, remains **abstract and metadata only**.
