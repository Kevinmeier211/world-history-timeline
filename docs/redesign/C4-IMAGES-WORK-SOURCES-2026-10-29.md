## Teotihuacan: sampled households and different uses of space

Broad monument/apartment ranges, reduced population after 600, master-plan alternatives, uncertain market function and the limited excavated residential sample retain their scope. ### The composite map’s rights and reading limits

**David M. Carballo, Luis Barba Pingarrón, Agustin Ortiz, Jorge Blancas, A. Gabriel Vicencio, Daniela Hernández Sariñana and Jorge Nukyen Archer Velasco, 2026, Figure 1, DOI 10.1017/laq.2026.10207. Cambridge University Press for the Society for American Archaeology. Based on the Teotihuacan Mapping Project (Millon, Drewitt and Cowgill 1973), with PATT fieldwork and Widmer/Storey 1993 modifications.**

The supplied **1650×2345 PNG** was downloaded and its actual pixels inspected; it is converted losslessly to WebP without cropping, enhancement or changed pixels. The upper panel has a north arrow and 1 km scale; the lower Tlajinga detail has a 200 m scale. It places the district south of the city core, Compounds 17/18 east of the Street of the Dead, the platform group west and San Lorenzo River to the north. It prints no geographic coordinate grid. This is composite archaeological mapping, not one ancient occupation or a measured plan of a single household. The drawing date remains unknown despite the 2026 publication date.

The publisher’s generated Long Description misnumbers or misnames several legend entries. Actual pixels and the scholarly caption take precedence: Tlajinga is **7**, Teopancazco **6**. Small original labels require enlargement and a readable text equivalent; they are not claimed to meet the typography floor for new original guides at their printed size. The captions preserve the key spatial relationships, composite chronology, rights and credit. No missing compound coordinates, invented north arrow, route or political boundary is added.

## Moche: objects, excavated work and regional arguments

Hélène Bernier’s [Moche Decorated Ceramics](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/moche-decorated-ceramics), source `moche`, is dated **1 August 2009**. Its selected main body was reread from geography/labor through making, imagery and domestic/funerary use. The linked books and underlying excavation reports were not read. This supplies a bounded account of specialized making, wear/repair and residential use without treating every vessel as grave-only or every scene as eyewitness evidence.

The selected [snake bottle](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/316721), `met-316721` / **1992.60.9**, and [seated-figure bottle](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/307474), `met-307474` / **82.1.30**, retain their original date ranges, order, photographs and rights. Visible descriptions/details and Public Domain statements were renewed, and the saved photographs were inspected. Mythological identity/renewal, rank/profession and possible former attachments remain suggestions. Neither record supplies an excavated valley/workshop. Provenance-tab custody text was not exposed; the API and linked current-image requests did not establish fresh remote-original byte parity.

The four new research records are deliberately scoped:

- **`moche-production-2009`** — Bernier, [La producción especializada de la cerámica doméstica y ritual Mochica](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-10432009000100010&script=sci_arttext), _Estudios Atacameños_ **37 (2009), 157–178**, DOI **10.4067/S0718-10432009000100010**. Figure pixels and underlying reports/experiments were not comprehensively consulted; no image/plan licence was verified or reproduction made.
- **`moche-households-2009`** — Claude Chapdelaine, [Domestic Life in and around the Urban Sector of the Huacas of Moche Site, Northern Peru](https://www.iia.unam.mx/sites/default/files/archivos/MANL510125/2009_ManzanillaChapdelaine-DomesticLifePrehispanicCapitals.pdf), chapter 9, pp.181–196, in _Domestic Life in Prehispanic Capitals_ (2009). These sources explain positive activities and changing institutions while distinguishing northern/southern and intra-valley differences. They do not assign either museum bottle to an excavated workshop, identify every worker’s status/payment or establish every household’s beliefs.
