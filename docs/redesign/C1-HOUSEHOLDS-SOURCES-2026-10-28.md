## Old Kingdom: provision, administration and service

Tara Prakash’s 2019 Met essay, [Egypt in the Old Kingdom](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/egypt-in-the-old-kingdom-ca-2649-2150-b-c), was read again in its title and full Dynasty 3–6 body. The current displayed convention is **c. 2649–2130 BCE**, while the URL retains an older endpoint. The stable dossier’s complete **−2649…−2130 interval and note** remain intact. The essay supplies period orientation, changing royal/elite funerary monuments, officials and debated provincial/political change. Funerary selection, precise reign chronologies, a single explanation of political change and the essay’s wider bibliography are not silently converted into comprehensive social history.

The Met’s [West wall of the chapel of Nikauhor and Sekhemhathor](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/543914), accession **08.201.2a–g**, was read in its description, Artwork Details, audio transcript and rights statement. No audio was played. The existing **1600 × 630** image was inspected without replacing or changing its bytes. Its **c. 2465–2389 BCE** catalogue date and **Saqqara, Tomb QS 915** provenance are separate from the wider period and the Giza evidence. The account retains offering niches, the couple’s identified roles, a displaced stela and an erased scene. Senet as a possible afterlife journey is the museum’s interpretation. Offerings intended to sustain the dead are presented as the chapel’s historical purpose, rather than an endorsed supernatural mechanism. The present wall is neither an intact original room nor a household census.

Three Ancient Egypt Research Associates accounts were consulted in their main text:

- [The Lost City of the Pyramids](https://aeraweb.org/projects/lost-city/): Fourth Dynasty settlement, craft/provisioning and administrative spaces, silos, seasonal transport, dismantling and gallery captions. A possible association with Khufu remains qualified. Linked excavation reports were not all read.
- [Feeding Pyramid Workers](https://aeraweb.org/feeding-pyramid-workers/): bakery molds, vats, hearths, ash, fuel, repeated small production units and the **1993 modern bakery experiment**, including its recipe/mold limitations. Remains, archaeological interpretation and experimental replication are separate evidence. The account does not establish an exact ancient recipe, every worker’s diet or individual recruitment status.
- [Khentkawes Town and the Menkaure Valley Temple](https://aeraweb.org/projects/kkt-mvt/): Fourth–Sixth Dynasty orientation, proposed movement from construction support toward cult service, three temple occupation phases, flooding, repair, reuse, basin/silo complex and cattle longbones in earlier excavation spoil. Resident identities, the bones’ interpretation as offerings passed to staff and climatic links remain qualified project interpretations. No exact year is assigned to every phase and no uniform or climate-only ending is asserted.

The [English annex to Pierre Tallet’s _Les papyrus de la mer Rouge I_](https://www.ifao.egnet.net/uploads/publications/divers/MIFAO136_ann_01.pdf), **MIFAO 136, 2017**, was consulted at printed **pp. 149–154 / PDF pp. 5–10**, with title/back-cover metadata. Colin Clement translated this annex; no Egyptian original, complete French volume or complete English annex reading is claimed. Printed **p. 150 / PDF p. 6** was inspected visually. Its restoration brackets and a restored return clause matter: the food entry supports a **mission to obtain provisions from Heliopolis**, without making a restored completed delivery certain. The late-Khufu crew journal records movement and administration; it does not explain raising every pyramid block or establish each worker’s freedom. **Merer’s crew is not identified as Heit el-Ghurab’s residents.** No papyrus pixels or translated passage are reproduced. The saved annex hash is `a6d1fa42d890eeee702066c0a5f34a7b1f5cb68ffe26a3393bfc5025aa8f3a44`.

Wadi el-Jarf’s institutional site page was a contextual lead, not an additional fully consulted corpus account. A large AERA settlement/cemetery PDF was refused by the web tool and is not treated as read. Household recruitment, farming, gender, provincial variation, critical original-language administrative interpretation and detailed labor/food quantities remain research tasks.

## Caral: construction, residence and differently sampled food evidence

UNESCO’s [Sacred City of Caral-Supe](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1269/) was renewed in Description, Brief synthesis and Integrity/Authenticity. It supplies architecture, settlement orientation and **3000–1800 BCE** development. Statements about state formation, hierarchy and priority remain institutional interpretations. They are not a recovered household roster or a ranking of civilizations. The stable dossier’s full interval, note and separate **1900 BCE endpoint** alternative remain unchanged. The Peruvian government page returned **418** on renewal: its previously consulted orientation retains its previous access date, and is not described as newly read.

Identifying particular residents as high-status officials is an inference from remains, not an excavated list of names/offices. Tribute, kinship and exact state-formation claims are not adopted as contemporary testimony. Construction bags do not by themselves establish coercion or ancient earthquake performance.

Yseki, Pezo-Lanfranco, Machacuay, Novoa and Shady’s [2023 dental-calculus study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41015-6), _Scientific Reports_ **13, 14143**, was read through selected publisher HTML: Introduction, Results, Discussion, Conclusions and Methods, including Study area, Burial contexts and extraction. Its **20 teeth represent nine Áspero individuals and one Caral individual**. Only **three burial contexts** were directly AMS-dated; others used contextual chronology. The Caral individual **CAR 391** is separately dated **2398–2038 cal BCE**. The Study area’s **2860–1970 cal BCE** Caral orientation is recorded without replacing the preserved UNESCO/government conventions.

Starch counts and sample ubiquity are not meal proportions or population dietary percentages. The study does not establish maize-beer fermentation. Linked tables, supplement and figure pixels were not independently inspected in full; no article image or micrograph is reused. The article is **CC BY 4.0**, subject to individual credit exclusions. A regional 2013 maize study consulted as a lead sampled **Pativilca/Fortaleza**, not Caral/Supe; its results are not transferred to this dossier. A university report on modern shicra-inspired engineering is not proof of every ancient building’s seismic performance.

The large UNESCO nomination and newer project PDFs were not retrieved through the web tool; the 2001 _Science_ page, selected residential papers and other blocked sources are documented in the preflight matrix. They are not full-text consultations. Wider household obligations, alternative archaeological interpretations, more complete food datasets and a rights-cleared measured construction study remain open.

## Skara Brae: phases, altered interiors and a regional landscape

Historic Environment Scotland’s [2024 _Statement of Significance_](https://app-hes-pubs-prod-neu-01.azurewebsites.net/api/file/703982e8-be07-4f7a-9265-b11e0121c461) was consulted in selected text: printed **pp. 15–17 / PDF 17–19**, **pp. 60–62 / PDF 62–64**, **pp. 67–69 / PDF 69–71**, plus copyright on **PDF p. 2**. Actual pixels on PDF **2, 17, 19 and 70** were inspected. The **89-page**, **7,044,627-byte** file has SHA-256 `594aa675bd8b2ed74c71b6a824420ae4b259e7cee1178cc85a6795f0c251a9ce`. This is not a complete bibliography, all excavation reports or every figure review.

The statement differentiates modeled boundaries: earlier activity beginning within **3360–3160 cal BCE**, earliest surviving houses beginning **2920–2885**, apparent interruption associated with **2870–2760**, renewed occupation within **2840–2685**, and later occupation ending **2545–2440 cal BCE**. Beginning/ending uncertainties are **not phase durations** or exact nonoverlapping reign-like periods. The new **3300–2440 BCE reference orientation** uses the existing atlas’s left display edge; **3300 is not a foundation date** and the earlier beginning range can precede it. Occupation is not presented as uninterrupted. The original **c. 3180–2500 BCE case and complete conflict note remain unchanged**.

The main source qualifies simplified claims: passages developed from gaps and some received roofing later; Structure 8’s date/function are uncertain; the ancient coast differed; regional monument association does not prove common institutions or full contemporaneity. Brodgar’s overlap with village occupation remains specifically uncertain.

Printed **pp. 67–69** describe House 1’s modern rebuilding. Nineteenth-century work omitted two recorded cells, introduced a window and may have rearranged shelving slabs. Modern positions of querns/potlids do not establish their ancient locations. The drawing/text on **printed p. 68 / PDF p. 70** distinguishes **four side slab enclosures, two per side, plus a fifth wider enclosure left of the entrance**, with a central hearth and shelving beyond. This source drawing was inspected to check the factual count and relationships, not traced or reproduced. It has a courtesy/permission caption. The guide avoids declaring every enclosure a confidently identified bed or inventing occupants, gender roles, measured walls, orientation or roof form.

Bishop and colleagues’ [_Scotland’s first farmers: new insights into early farming practices in North-west Europe_](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/scotlands-first-farmers-new-insights-into-early-farming-practices-in-northwest-europe/7C027B8A129F68533E558FAB47B63027), _Antiquity_ **96(389), 1087–1104**, **14 September 2022**, was read in abstract, introductory method overview and the Skara Brae/Braes of Ha’Breck section, with metadata/licence checks. Its small **underlying Phase 0** grain assemblage precedes the principal surviving houses. Mostly naked barley, some emmer/hulled barley and the low/moderate-manuring interpretation remain tied to that selected evidence. **Ha’Breck’s large burned grain deposit and extreme isotope results are not assigned to Skara.** The article’s main-house boundary ranges differ slightly from HES 2024; the reference uses HES’s ranges rather than silently merging them. Supplement, individual table rows and figure pixels were not independently inspected. No precise sample/outcome value or figure is reproduced. The article is CC BY 4.0.

The [HES visitor account](https://www.historicenvironment.scot/visit/all/skara-brae/history-and-stories/) was renewed for houses, artifacts and ending, while its general covered-passage/workshop wording is qualified above. UNESCO’s [Heart of Neolithic Orkney](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/514/) Description and Brief synthesis supply settlement, tomb and circle orientation, without identifying one government or the same builders. The old tool URL now redirects to [Trove object 9194](https://www.trove.scot/object/9194?rsrc=historicenvironment.scot): Summary and Further details were read. Its current numeric **Date made 3179 to 2498** differs from the prior **3180–2500** structured values; its summary still gives **3100–2400 BC**. No calendar-migration or off-by-one explanation is invented. The sandstone **X.HA.668** function remains uncertain, and the National Museums Scotland loan is retained. Its image requires permission and was neither copied nor visually inspected in this pass.

A Ness of Brodgar house account and historical plan records were contextual leads, not a full excavation-report or reusable-plan clearance. A rights-cleared measured site plan, fuller archaeological criticism and more extensive household/landscape evidence remain tasks.
