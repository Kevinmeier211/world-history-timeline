`Subject.interval` still describes the bounded investigation. New `HistoricalContext` records carry their own dates, caveats, citations and phases. Subjects refer to them by `contextIds`. Four contexts demonstrate the distinction:

- Rome: 27 BCE–284 CE is an early imperial orientation, ending with Diocletian's accession; the Severan case remains 193–235. Neither boundary is the lifespan of Rome.
- Han: 206 BCE–220 CE includes the explicitly identified Xin interruption. Eastern Han remains 25–220. The 178 BCE memorial and 81 BCE debate are earlier evidence, not direct descriptions of 200 CE policy.
- Angkor: approximate ninth–fifteenth-century capital-era orientation, with continued habitation and religious use beyond that frame. Phase labels are broad construction/occupation periods.

## Reading standard to carry into later work

For each proposed major entry, check the following against the actual reader, not just its JSON:

1. Can a newcomer place this inquiry in time, geography and a longer sequence without confusing an object date with a society's lifespan?
2. Does the main account explain people doing things: producing, governing, maintaining, negotiating, moving or remembering? Does it explain a mechanism and change, beyond repeatedly saying evidence has limits?
3. Can the reader identify a specific source, its audience/purpose, what it supports and a consequential limit? Are quotations, paraphrases and interpretations distinguished?
4. Does a map/image/document answer a historical question at its displayed size? Is the text equivalent useful? Is the caption explicit about modern observation, reconstruction and uncertainty?
5. Is the family activity concrete and answerable from the supplied material? Does the adult path add interpretation rather than repeat the summary?
6. Is there a meaningful comparison or contextual destination and a reliable way back? Does print retain the explanation, evidence and source limitations?
7. Are source consultation, rights and unresolved work recorded without treating an automated pass as scholarly approval?

## Deliberate limits

The global overview has 23 authored anchors in seven regional rows and only four broad reference contexts. This establishes the interaction and date semantics, not a complete world chronology. Around 1950, nearby cases are explicitly dated and described as nearby; several regions still lack contemporary reference coverage. Selecting 1950 is useful without making those cases simultaneous.

World maps support zoom, pan, reset and grouped 44-pixel selection targets. Local place studies are sourced orientation diagrams; they are not surveyed medieval plans, reconstructed political boundaries or invented migration routes. The Punjab historical document is separately identified and can be enlarged.
