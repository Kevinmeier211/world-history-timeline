# C2 material work: consultation, rights and remaining research

Local author consultation on **28 October 2026** for **Olmec communities, Lapita and Poverty Point**. This consolidates the selected source work recorded in `output/c2-coasts-preflight-2026-10-28/` and the isolated authoring modules. It is not independent historical approval, a claim that every linked work was read, or a validation/release receipt. The earlier Hattusha/Uluburun/Shang C2 handoff remains a separate checkpoint.

The source distinctions are central to this set. A modern plaster cast is not an ancient sherd; a LiDAR surface is not a single ancient construction phase; a dated layer is not a recovered voyage; and a modern population estimate is not an ancient census. The original complete case intervals remain **c. 1200–400 BCE** for Olmec, **c. 1500–500 BCE** for Lapita and **c. 1700–1100 BCE** for the main Poverty Point earthwork complex. Reference contexts have their own qualified frames.

## Olmec: small figures, buried floors and different regional centres

James Doyle’s February 2017 Met essay, [Olmec Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/olmec-art), was read again in its main body: the artistic frame, San Lorenzo earth building and pottery, El Manatí, changing prominent centres, La Venta platforms and greenstone offerings, and continuing production elsewhere. Further-reading books and linked excavation reports were not read. Cultural/artistic labels do not establish one state, inevitable ancestry, a single regional chronology or a universal “mother culture.”

The [Female Figure](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/314609), accession **1983.424**, was checked in Description, Artwork Details, Public Domain and Open Access information. The existing **821 × 1600** image was actually inspected. Its **1200–900 BCE** catalogue range, ceramic material and **17 cm** height belong to this object; the record does **not** supply an excavated findspot. Ear ornaments, coiffure and modeled bodily details are visible. Status, bodily modification, life-stage and ritual readings are attributed museum/scholarly interpretations, including the catalogue’s comparisons with Tlatilco and discussions of Joyce and Marcus. The photograph does not identify a San Lorenzo household, a named person or a universal figurine use. The existing image retains Met Open Access/CC0 rights and its original credit; catalogue prose has separate rights.

Virginia Arieta Baizabal’s [2020 demographic-method article](https://scielo.org.mx/pdf/educm/v35n1/2448-6515-educm-35-01-117.pdf), _Estudios Demográficos y Urbanos_ **35(1), 117–152**, DOI **10.24201/edu.v35i1.1851**, was read in selected text at printed **pp. 130, 132, 134–135 and 138–142**. The source-specific construction scheme—**Arenas c. 1800–1400, Terrazas c. 1400–1200, and BAV c. 1200–1000 BCE**—is separate from the case interval and later museum peak conventions. Exact population, rank, labor obligations and a sole demographic-collapse explanation are not adopted. The article carries **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**; no source map, plotted sample, figure or geometry is copied, traced or adapted for the original teaching guide.

### A modern cast is not an ancient sherd

The Met’s [Cast of a Pot Sherd](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/314215), accession **1980.153.2**, was read in Artwork Details and rights information, and its **1056 × 775** image was actually inspected. The catalogue dates the **plaster reproduction to the mid-twentieth century**, gives Santa Cruz Islands/Temotu province geography, and credits **Rogers Fund, 1980**. The acquisition year is not a manufacture year. No original sherd’s exact date, excavation locus or named cast maker is supplied in the consulted record.

The image is Met Open Access/CC0. Any use must visibly identify the photographed material as a modern cast. Its catalogue wording does not justify invented numeric endpoints such as 1940–1960: numeric bounds remain unknown, with the verbal mid-century date retained. The visible pattern is evidence of what the reproduction shows, not proof of an ancient object’s precise findspot or voyage.

A Te Papa sherd record was investigated but its indexed rights state all rights reserved/copyright holder sought; no image was downloaded or copied. An inaccessible 2023 article and an unavailable Donohue/Denham repository record were not treated as full-text sources. A selected older ANU chapter section was read as a lead but not adopted in place of the later Tongan studies.

## Poverty Point: earthwork parts, food and earlier related practices

The [NPS main article](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/monumental-earthworks-of-poverty-point-world-heritage-site.htm), last updated **23 July 2020**, was read in its full main account and saved. It supplies the raised setting, **1700–1100 BCE** residential/ceremonial frame, food/material orientation and continuing research. Exact earth-volume/distance/priority claims and speculative capital/status language are not adopted. Its Susan Guice lead photograph is not assumed public domain simply because NPS hosts it; it is not reused.

The [Louisiana Division of Archaeology booklet](https://www.crt.state.la.us/dataprojects/archaeology/povertypoint/assets/poverty-point-pdf-1.0.pdf), **1 October 2014**, has **32 pages**, with printed and PDF numbering matching. Actual pixels were inspected at **pp. 2, 4, 7, 10–13, 17, 24–25 and 32**; the trade/travel text on **pp. 5–6** was also read. This is selected consultation, not a whole-book or full excavation-report claim. The credits identify writers **Robert Connolly, Diana Greenlee, Nancy Hawkins, Chip McGimsey and Joel Zovar**.

- **Food is p. 7**, including poor bone preservation; pottery and PPO cooking belong to **pp. 24–25**. Pages 22–23 concern different artifact classes. The earlier loose locator is not carried forward as a precise food citation.
- **Mound A** is rounded to **1350 BCE on p. 4** and **1300 BCE on p. 10**. These passages do not establish one exact construction date. Peak, platform and ramp have a sequence; a proposed rapid platform episode is not a measured duration for all of Mound A or the entire site.
- **Mound B/C material on pp. 12–13** is in two columns that interleave in extraction. Claims were checked against actual pixels rather than assigned by text order alone.
- **Mound D around 700 CE**, old artifacts redeposited in its later fill, nineteenth-century road damage to Mound C and later cemetery/farm use remain separate from the main-case interval. **Page 17** supplies plaza filling, gully control and posts, bringing continuing care into the account.

The booklet states **© 2014 Louisiana Division of Archaeology** and gives individual photograph, painting and map credits. Its Herb Roe reconstruction sequence and other illustrations are consulted, not copied, traced or adapted. Grooms, Ward and Kidder’s [2023 primary study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/convergence-at-poverty-point-a-revised-chronology-of-the-late-archaic-lower-mississippi-valley/121431E2E3469A819185F44BF7AB81EA), _Antiquity_ **97(396), 1453–1469**, DOI **10.15184/aqy.2023.155**, was published online **25 October 2023**. The small embedded legend requires a readable caption/text equivalent; the unaltered source figure must not be claimed to meet the 10 pt floor applied to newly authored diagrams. No newly recovered detail is claimed. Final display/print suitability belongs to the separate actual-output review.

The two original guides explain **how an Olmec household estimate depends on dating/grouping/analogy** and **how repeated stamping actions build a design**. They use independently arranged teaching symbols, without copying source maps, excavated floor outlines, authentic motifs or a measured ancient tool. Their 2026 creation dates, represented evidence and CC BY 4.0 artwork rights remain separate from the historical sources and photographed objects. The preserved figure, modern cast and terrain image have their own item-specific rights described above; a common topic does not confer a common licence.

This set uses comparison to investigate material work, provisioning and changing places. It establishes no historical contact, shared language, identical governing system or single-origin sequence linking Olmec, Lapita and Poverty Point. Complete excavation reports, original experimental studies, fuller dietary datasets, supplementary model auditing and community-specific interpretations remain research tasks. Independent source/explanation review and actual family, screen-reader, device and physical-print assessment remain separate from the authors’ checks.
