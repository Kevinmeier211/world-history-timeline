## Chronology, inference and remaining research

The three original case intervals remain: Hattusha -1300 to -1200, Uluburun -1335 to -1305, Shang -1600 to -1046. Separate reference frames cover selected Hattusha settlement/capital orientation (-2000 to -1180), eastern Mediterranean exchange (-1500 to -1200) and the conventional Shang frame (-1600 to -1046). The new Hattusha reference permits discussion of the later drought without stretching its original case. These windows do not claim complete settlement or network lifespans.

Yin Xu’s late-capital dates are narrower than Shang’s broader convention. The gong’s numerical -1300 to -1001 search envelope expresses the museum’s 13th–11th-century label; it is not an assertion of production in every included year. The museum separately assigns the object to the Shang period. Its qualified Anyang attribution is retained.

The new journey uses thematic order, not a route or one global collapse. No new historical relationship is inferred. A cargo association, chemical source argument, environmental proxy and museum attribution require different contextual evidence.

Full residential reports, household food distribution, labor/gender/coercion, ship ownership/intermediaries, disputed metal provenance, translated inscriptions, workshops and domestic contexts remain targeted work. Independent historical review should assess explanatory quality and source use. Real adult/family use, screen readers, physical devices and physical print remain separate acceptance tasks.
